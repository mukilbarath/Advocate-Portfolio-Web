P084 (style=Normal): 3. Literature Review, Statutory Regulatory Framework & Judicial Technology
P085 (style=Normal): 3.1 The Advocates Act, 1961 & Section 49(1)(c)
P086 (style=Normal): The legal profession in India is statutorily governed by The Advocates Act, 1961 (Act No. 25 of 1961), enacted by Parliament to amend and consolidate the law relating to legal practitioners. Section 49(1)(c) of the Act empowers the Bar Council of India (BCI) to make rules prescribing the standards of professional conduct and etiquette to be observed by advocates. Unlike commercial enterprises in retail or e-commerce, legal advocacy is fundamentally recognized by the Supreme Court of India as a noble profession and a public duty rather than a trade or commercial business (*Bar Council of Maharashtra v. M.V. Dabholkar, 1976 2 SCC 291*). Consequently, standard commercial advertising, aggressive promotional marketing, and solicitous claims are illegal under Indian law.
P087 (style=Normal): 3.2 Bar Council of India Rule 36 Legal Advertisement Norms & Technical Compliance
P088 (style=Normal): Rule 36 of Section VI of the Bar Council of India Rules strictly prohibits advocates from advertising, soliciting work, or making unsubstantiated claims of superiority. Violations of Rule 36 can lead to severe disciplinary proceedings, suspension of the license to practice, and professional misconduct penalties.
P089 (style=Normal): In 2008, a landmark amendment to Rule 36 permitted advocates to publish basic, factual information on their websites, restricted to: (a) name and address, (b) telephone numbers and email, (c) academic qualifications, (d) enrollment details and court standing, and (e) recognized areas of practice. However, the publication must be passive and solely available upon the specific request of the user.
P090 (style=Normal): Our technical solution directly addressed this legal constraint by implementing two robust compliance features:
P091 (style=List Bullet): Mandatory User Consent Modal: A modal overlay prompts the user upon initial entry to acknowledge that they are visiting the website solely for informational purposes and that no solicitation or advocate-client relationship is created.
P092 (style=List Bullet): Permanent Statutory Disclaimer: A prominent, high-contrast disclaimer card is embedded in the website footer explicitly reciting Bar Council of India Rule 36.
